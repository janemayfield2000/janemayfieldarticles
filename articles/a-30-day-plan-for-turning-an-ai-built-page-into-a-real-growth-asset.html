A 30-Day Plan for Turning an AI-Built Page Into a Real Growth Asset

Launching a page is a moment; growing it is a process. Small teams often nail the first part and completely skip the second, which is why so many otherwise well-built pages plateau within weeks of going live.

A practical way to structure that process is a 30-day cycle. The first five days go toward planning: locking in one audience segment, one page objective, and a baseline structure with a fixed section order — no writing yet.

Days six through ten are for drafting and refining — generating AI-assisted section content, then manually tightening the language for specificity and trust. By day fifteen, the page should be live with tracking in place for one primary conversion event and one supporting behavior signal, nothing more complicated than that.

The following week is dedicated to the first real optimization cycle: reviewing behavior data and testing exactly one major variable, like a headline or CTA block. This entire cadence, including the final stabilization phase, is mapped out in detail in this practical execution model for new pages.

The last week of the cycle is about documentation — recording what changed, what improved, what's still unclear — and scheduling the next monthly review. That single habit is often what separates a page that keeps improving from one that quietly goes stale.
