What "DR or Awareness" Actually Means When Choosing Directory Targets

Directory submission goals usually fall into one of two buckets, and conflating them leads to a target list that serves neither well. One goal is domain authority — building backlink value from higher-DR sites to support SEO. The other is awareness — getting discovered by real users browsing those directories, regardless of the site's authority metrics.

A directory optimized for DR might be a dry, link-farm-adjacent site with almost no organic traffic. A directory optimized for awareness might have modest authority but an actively engaged audience of exactly the users you want finding your product. Neither is inherently better — they just serve different objectives.

The mistake most founders make is not picking a goal at all, ending up with a mixed list that half-serves both purposes without excelling at either.

Filtering directory targets explicitly around one goal or the other leads to a noticeably sharper, more effective target list than a generic "submit everywhere" approach.

Before your next submission wave, decide first: are you optimizing for link equity, or for eyeballs? The answer should shape your entire target list.
