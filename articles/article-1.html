Treat Directory Submissions Like a Project, Not a To-Do List Item

Founders often approach directory submissions the same way they approach a quick errand — knock it out in an afternoon, move on, forget about it. That mindset tends to produce sloppy, inconsistent results across platforms, because directory work actually behaves more like an ongoing project than a one-time task.

The core problem with the errand mindset is that it skips tracking. Without a record of which platforms you've submitted to, which login credentials you used, and what description you wrote where, founders quickly lose the ability to manage their own footprint. Six months later, updating a company description or fixing a broken link becomes a scavenger hunt instead of a five-minute task, simply because there's no central record of what exists.

A simple system solves most of this. A spreadsheet with columns for platform name, submission date, account email, live URL, and last-updated date takes minutes to set up and saves hours of confusion later. This is especially important for founders working across multiple products or brands, where it's easy to lose track of which listing belongs to which company without a clear system in place.

Treating this as a project also means thinking about cadence rather than a single burst of effort. Rather than submitting to every relevant platform in one exhausting week, spacing submissions into planned waves — tied to launch milestones, feature releases, or quarterly check-ins — tends to produce more consistent, higher-quality listings than a single rushed sprint.

This project mindset pays off most clearly when something changes — a rebrand, a pivot, a new URL structure. Founders with a tracked system can update every relevant listing methodically; founders without one often just let outdated information sit indefinitely, quietly undermining the credibility those listings were supposed to build in the first place.
