Keep, Tune, or Replace: A Simple Framework for GPU Decisions

Every GPU owner eventually faces the same three-way fork: keep running the current card, tune it for a bit more life, or replace it outright. Without a clear framework, this decision tends to get made emotionally — either replacing too early out of launch-day hype or waiting too long until reliability has already collapsed.

The "keep" case is straightforward: if your output targets are being met, thermals are stable, and incidents are rare, there's no reason to change anything beyond routine maintenance and ongoing trend monitoring.

"Tune" makes sense when performance is close to target but margins are shrinking. This is the point to prioritize airflow cleanup, conservative voltage settings, and workload optimization before spending money on new hardware — cheap fixes often close the gap entirely.

"Replace" becomes the right call when reliability risk keeps climbing or your core targets simply can't be hit without constant compromises. At that point, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper — in money and stress — than a reactive one triggered by an actual failure.

Running this evaluation on a fixed schedule, rather than only when something breaks, is what actually makes the framework useful. This keep-tune-replace decision guide includes a monthly and quarterly review structure designed to catch the right moment to switch categories.

The goal isn't finding a perfect card — it's building a decision process you can trust every time the question comes up.
